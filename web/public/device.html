EMO

A small round thing you wear. You talk to it. Your work gets done.

Tap. Talk. Done.

One tap on the glass and it is listening. Say the thing the way you would say it to a person. Tap again and it is gone from your shoulders and into your brain, sorted, with a date and an owner.

Nothing to type. Nothing to file. Nothing to remember afterwards.

Say what kind of thing it is, before you say it.

Turn the rim with your thumb before you speak. A task. A reminder. A question. A meeting. The story of your day. Something to go away and research. Fourteen in all, and each one arrives as a different kind of thing at the other end.

You are not recording audio. You are saying what you mean, and it lands already understood.

It asks. You answer.

When something genuinely needs you, it appears on the glass and waits. A person who says a job is finished. A worker of yours that will not spend anything until you say so.

Hold the glass and answer in one sentence. The decision is made and the work moves, and you never opened anything.

Your workers, worn.

Turn the rim to Agent and describe a job the way you would describe it to a new person. Every night at eleven, read my mail and write me a page. Every Friday, ask the team for next week's numbers. Chase anyone who has gone quiet for three days.

It works out what it needs, wires it together, tests it and runs it from then on. You never open it again. The only time you hear from it is when it needs a decision it is not willing to make for you.

Basements. Lifts. Factory floors.

No signal, so it keeps what you said and sends it the moment it can. The screen tells you how many are still waiting. Nothing you say is ever lost, whatever the network is doing.

A screen that is dark on purpose.

On this display black is not a colour, it is off. The face you see almost all day is a few white letters floating on nothing. It is dark because it is doing nothing to you.

It hears you, not the room.

Two microphones and an echo-cancelling front end, made for the car park, the corridor, the site, the café at lunchtime. You speak normally. It takes your voice out of the noise.

Wear it and forget it.

Leave it alone for five minutes and it does not sleep, it switches off completely. Press it and it is awake before your thumb leaves the glass.

Machined aluminium, fifty-five millimetres across and fifteen thick, with a lanyard through the case. No buttons to learn: press to wake, tap to speak, the rim to choose, one button that always means go back.

It improves itself.

New versions arrive on their own and install overnight. You wake up to a better device than the one you wore yesterday.

Next · your voice, already typed

Stop typing. Anywhere.

Hold the glass and talk. The words appear where you are already writing — the mail, the document, the reply you have been avoiding for three days — punctuated, in your own phrasing, as fast as you can think it.

Typing becomes the slow way to write.

    Next · a worker with its own computer

    Give the work away, out loud.

    Describe the job once, on the way to the car, and hand it over. A worker with a machine of its own opens the applications, fills the forms, moves between the sites and finishes the thing, the way a person would.

    Show it once and it keeps the method for good. It comes back only when a decision needs a human — and that arrives on your chest, not in a queue.

    Neither is finished. They are named here because they are what this object is being built towards, and because you should know where it is going before you decide to wear one.

    Specifications.

    Display
    Round AMOLED, 1.75 inch, 466 × 466, 700 nits. Black pixels are off.
    Audio
    Dual MEMS microphone array with an echo-cancelling front end.
    Battery
    500 mAh, with charging and a fuel gauge on board. True full power-off, not sleep.
    Wireless
    Wi-Fi and Bluetooth 5.
    Port
    USB-C.
    Case
    CNC machined aluminium, 55 mm across, 15 mm thick, lanyard fitted.
    Controls
    Press to wake. Tap to speak. Turn the rim to choose. One button to go back.
    Setup
    It makes its own network and takes your Wi-Fi once.
    Updates
    Installed over the air, on their own.
