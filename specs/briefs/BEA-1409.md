# BEA-1409 — Brief First 5/6: the app learns each tool by trying it

Part five of **Brief First**. Plan: <https://claude.ai/code/artifact/5c17fc65-a717-43b3-9bc6-4221968b0d94>
Linear: <https://linear.app/beakn/issue/BEA-1409>
Needs 1/6 (BEA-1405).

## Why

The owner asked the sharpest question of the whole discussion: *"How does Codex know about all the
tools we are using and the actions each tool has?"*

Three sources feed it. Two are automatic and complete — the vendor's own schema, and real saved
answers (`ToolSample`). The third is **35 hand-written notes in `api/src/tools/knowledge-notes.ts`**,
against thousands of available actions. The Gmail trap (`max_results` defaults to 1) is known only
because it cost the owner a night and then a human typed it in. That loop is what he wants ended: a
tool nobody has used has no notes at all, so the first agent on it is guaranteed to be wrong, and he
ends up back in Claude Code.

## Build

**`ToolLesson`** — learned facts, written by the app, never by hand:
`actionId · key · text · evidenceSampleId · firstSeenAt · lastConfirmedAt · confidence · timesSeen`.
Unique on `(actionId, key)`.

After every real call (the same place `ToolSample` is written), compare what was **asked for** with
what **came back**, and record what it teaches:

- **Silent default** — a count parameter was left out and exactly 1 (or the vendor's stated default)
  came back, while a paging cursor says there is more. *This is the Gmail bug, learned automatically.*
- **There is more** — the answer carries a next-page cursor/token; record how paging works and what a
  page holds.
- **A cap was hit** — asked for N, got exactly the vendor's maximum.
- **Quietly ignored** — an argument that was sent but is absent from the echoed request, or had no
  effect on the answer.
- **Shape surprises** — a single object where a list was expected (the BEA-1377 failure), or a field
  that is always empty.

Lessons are **merged into the fact card** beside the hand notes, marked as learned, with the number of
times seen. They go to Codex in the build payload and to the builder during the interview.

A lesson that stops being true (the vendor changed) is **demoted, not silently kept** —
`lastConfirmedAt` ages out and confidence drops.

**A lesson is never invented by an LLM.** Every one is derived mechanically from a request/answer pair
and carries the sample id that proves it. The hand notes stay, for the traps only a human knows.

## Acceptance (WHEN / MUST)

- WHEN `gmail.fetch_emails` is called with no `max_results` and one email comes back with a next-page
  token, a "silent default" lesson MUST be written **without any human input**, and MUST name the
  parameter.
- WHEN the same lesson is seen again, `timesSeen` MUST rise and no duplicate row MUST be created.
- WHEN a lesson has not been confirmed for its ageing window, its confidence MUST drop.
- WHEN a fact card is built, learned lessons MUST appear in it, marked as learned, with their
  evidence.
- WHEN a brand-new action is called for the first time, its card MUST already carry whatever that call
  taught — with zero hand-written notes.
- Every lesson MUST carry a `ToolSample` id that exists.
- NO lesson MUST ever be produced by a model.
- Vault actions and message-body actions MUST teach nothing (they are never sampled).

## Notes

- Derived mechanically. No AI cost.
- Plain English in the lesson text — it is shown to the owner on the tool card.
